002Why a character

Every other bag on that shelf
is a photograph of a cat
nobody knows.

Photography can only ever show a stranger's cat. It cannot wave, it cannot be sad when the bowl is empty, and it cannot walk into a shop on a Saturday morning. A drawn character can do all three — and it costs nothing to redraw him for the next campaign, the next flavour, the next country.

Larry already exists in every state a brand needs. That is the whole argument: the expensive part is finished.

Recognition

A face reads faster than a name

Shoppers spot the orange cat before they read "Larry". The character becomes the shelf-block, not the logo.

Range

One asset, endless posts

Hungry, smug, sleepy, surprised. Sixty ready expressions is a social calendar that never needs a photoshoot.

Reach

He leaves the screen

A drawing becomes a costume, an inflatable, a plush, a sticker. A photograph becomes none of those.

006Two routes · pick one

Same cat.
Two different businesses.

These are not two colourways. They sell to different people, in different shops, at different prices. Switch between them:

Loud, and proud of it.

Larry is the hero — full body, front and centre, big enough to read from three metres down a supermarket aisle. Bright blue and orange do the shouting. This route wins impulse buys and moves fast on social.

LARRY

Quiet, and expensive.

The monogram leads and Larry steps back to become a small signature in the corner. Olive, gold and deep black. This route lives on a counter rather than a shelf, and it earns its margin from restraint.

Sells where

Wordmark

Risk
The cartoon cat fights the serif — see the note below
One honest note before you choose. The premium route is not finished. Its serif monogram and gold palette were built for a refined line drawing, and the current Larry is a bold cartoon — put them together and they argue. If Route B is the direction, Larry needs redrawing in a thinner, calmer line first. Route A works today, exactly as the artwork stands.
